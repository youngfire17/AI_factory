## 2. Repeatable playbooks (7)
Every playbook is tagged [assumed] on cost and timing because the sources give outcomes, not budgets.

**P1. Free browser build as funnel, then paid Steam version.** (Roottrees, Buckshot, Die in the Dungeon, Webfishing, Cookie Clicker)
Steps: (1) 2-4 week game on itch with browser play and a tip jar; (2) watch plays/returns; (3) if it hits, make a polished Steam version with more content; (4) put the Steam page up months before launch; (5) pitch 5-10 journalists/creators personally with a named comparable. Timeline: itch Mo 0-3; Steam page Mo 3; launch Mo 9-12. Cost: $0 until Steam ($100 fee, recoupable at $1,000 revenue [verified, prior brief]). Working signals: itch front-page/featured, 1k+ browser plays/day, unsolicited streamers, 500+ wishlists/month.

**P2. Low-price survivors-like or "twist on a proven genre" with demo + festival.** (VS, Brotato, Megabonk, Ball x Pit)
Steps: demo in 3 months, public Steam page, enter Next Fest with a demo already out 1-2 months (Zukowski's data says pre-fest wishlists correlate 0.825 with festival wishlists; winners had demos out 6-12 months [claimed, search summary]), launch EA at $3-5. Targets: Brotato got 40k wishlists from two festivals [verified]. Our realistic target is lower; 2,000+ pre-fest wishlists is the rule of thumb [claimed]. Cost: $100 fee + $0-50 [assumed].

**P3. Clip-able moment first (streamer/TikTok bait).** (Buckshot, Lethal Company, Webfishing, Backpack Battles, Megabonk)
Steps: design the 15-second funny/tense moment before the systems; capture it automatically; seed 10-20 small creators 2 months before launch (Lethal Company did exactly this [verified]). Metric: clip views per post, creators who play unprompted.

**P4. Daily-seed shareable result.** (Wordle, Quordle, Framed)
Steps: one puzzle per day, emoji-grid copy button, no login; clone-with-a-twist of a hot format within weeks. Metric: share-button click rate, D7 return. Money: ads/donations; the exit was sale (Wordle low seven figures, Quordle). Revenue evidence for small versions is thin (Moviedle about $1k donations in 3 months [claimed, prior brief]).

**P5. Idle game that rewards long sessions, F2P with cosmetic supporter DLC.** (Idle Pixel Fantasy, Zero Stress King, Cookie Clicker, Antimatter Dimensions)
Steps: depth over polish, prestige layers, offline progress; on Steam, the F2P "Popular" shelf drives visibility from plays, not wishlists [claimed]. One-hit upside is real (IPF $4.8k in 5 days; ZSK ~$160k tracker estimate), but ~97.5% of sub-$5 idlers miss 1,000 reviews [claimed, prior brief].

**P6. Portfolio of small web games on portals.** (Emolingo/Poki, @anulagarwal)
Steps: ship a game every 1-2 weeks to itch, then CrazyGames/Poki/Playgama; double down on the one that retains. Money is ad share (Poki 50/50 on Poki-sourced traffic [claimed]); typical first-game range $500-$3,000/month is a vendor guide [claimed, weak]. Metric: portal CTR and average play time.

**P7. Jam prototype iterated in public, then port winners.** (Webfishing, Peak, Roottrees, Vampire Survivors mobile port)
Steps: weekend prototype -> devlog -> keep best -> after traction, port to mobile (VS did this in 3 weeks to beat clones) or console via a partner (Brotato used a third-party porter). Constraint: many jams ban AI art/audio (prior brief); use AI-permitted jams only.

## 3. Design traits of breakouts (from the 32 cases)
1. One-sentence hook ("a roguelike deckbuilder with poker hands", "russian roulette with a shotgun", "survivors but 3D").
2. A readable GIF/clip: the "wow" appears within 15 seconds (Buckshot, Webfishing, Lethal Company).
3. Time-to-fun under 2 minutes; Brotato shortened runs to waves.
4. Low price ($1.20-$5 for the true cheap-volume hits; $8-$10 for co-op) or free.
5. Systems depth under a simple surface (Balatro, idle prestige layers).
6. Juice: VS's slot-machine chest animations came from the dev's gambling-industry job [verified, Wikipedia].
7. Remix of a proven mechanic, not a new genre (VS from Magic Survival; Ball x Pit = Breakout + VS).

## 4. Pre-launch audience building: what they actually did, with numbers
- Balatro: 48 wishlists after beta month, 183 by 10 Jun 2023, then streamers and 3+ demo windows [claimed]. Not a big pre-launch audience.
- Brotato: 40k wishlists from two festival demos [verified].
- Roottrees: 8k wishlists after ~10 months, 13k at launch [verified].
- Ball x Pit: 88k wishlists from a top-10 Next Fest demo [claimed]. Tiny Kingdom: 10k+ wishlists in 6 months, itch feature ~250 [verified].
- Lethal Company: used the audience from earlier games and small playtests [verified]. Fly.pieter: 600k+ followers [claimed].
- Zukowski's Feb 2026 survey (182 devs): median ~800 wishlists over the festival week; 1,000-6,999 "respectable" for 1-2 person teams; 68-88% of festival wishlists come from people who never download the demo [claimed, search summary; his site not read].
- Takeaway: devlogs and Discords were not the common thread. The common thread was a free playable build that a stranger could try (itch/demo) plus a few creators.

## 5. Scaling into studios
- Vampire Survivors: hit Steam traction Jan-Feb 2022, quit job Feb 2022, hired freelancers Mar 2022 (6-8 weeks), Xbox Nov 2022, mobile Dec 2022 [verified]; by 2026 plans for several small teams, a pause on publishing, and 15+ projects [claimed, VGC/PSU].
- Brotato: planned to build a team after success [verified]; ports done by third party.
- Schedule I: 3 members, aiming for 4 [claimed, one source]. Balatro: signed with Playstack, then scaled back to part-time hobby pace [claimed].
- Emolingo: 2 -> 5 FT over years via a portal partnership [verified]. Adopt Me: 2 -> ~40 staff [claimed].
- Pattern: first hire within 2-3 months of breakout; sequel/DLC/ports/publisher deals follow. Nobody scaled before a hit.

## 6. AI-assisted games that earned, and disclosure
- Only Levels' Fly.pieter (AI-coded, ad revenue, audience pre-existing) and Infinite Craft (AI as the mechanic; revenue unknown). I could not verify any AI-made Steam game with real earnings. No AI-attributed review-bomb study found beyond the prior brief's Steam numbers.
- Vibe Jam 2026: 90%+ AI-written code, free web only; $25k/$10k/$5k prizes; judges say quality is near "a third-year student" [verified, levels.io; press release lists the prize pool]. Prize money is the only income documented.
- Handling: Valve requires disclosure of shipped AI content (prior brief). Our approach: procedural art, honest "AI-assisted code" note, lead with design, not with AI.

## 8. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Survivorship bias; hits are viral and unbuyable | Run 6+ cheap shots; kill by stage-gate numbers |
| AI disclosure drag on Steam reach [claimed] | Procedural art; lead on itch/portals; Steam only for a proven winner |
| Jam/portal bans on AI | Enter AI-permitted jams only; check each rule page |
| Clone saturation in survivors-like/idle | Pick a twist; demo early to test hook |
| No audience to seed launch | Free browser builds + personal pitches to small creators |
| Spend cap vs Steam $100 fee | Defer until itch signal; get explicit owner approval |

## Verdict
**Supports, with a different shape than "launch on Steam".** Documented tiny-team wins share one pattern: a free, easily tried build, a one-sentence clip-able hook, a low price, and a few creators. That fits agent-built small 2D games. Weak links: the cases with real money (Balatro, VS, Brotato, Lethal Company) took 1-3 years or an existing audience, and I found no verified AI-built game with real earnings.

**What would change my mind:** an AI-disclosed game gross >$10k on Steam; evidence that itch/portal featuring reaches strangers without an audience; or our own prototypes failing the 25% D1 threshold.

**Next cheapest test ($0):** ship 3 browser prototypes (survivors-like, idle, daily puzzle) to itch, log D1 and session time, and have the owner send 10 personalised creator DMs for the best one.
