## The core idea
Our binding limit is not build capacity (agents) but cash for testing and reach. Three partners lend us both:
1. Portals lend audience at zero cost (CrazyGames, Playgama, Poki).
2. Hyper/hybrid-casual publishers lend the first UA test budget and the scaling budget (Supersonic gives $300 for a CPI test [verified]).
3. Licensing buyers pay cash up front for finished web games.
Stage-gates cap our own spend at $75 until G3 and 25% of cash above the $250 reserve at G4 [verified, charter/stage-gates.md]. So a $200-300 fake-ad test is not self-fundable at G2/G3. Publisher-funded tests fit those caps. That decides the ordering of the stack below.

## 2. "Test before you build" (fake-ad / CPI test)
- Method: 4-5 short gameplay videos (10-15 s), Facebook/Meta, link-click or install campaign, point to a "coming soon" page or the store; run 4-5 days. Clap Clap Games spends $50/day, about $200-250 per test; CTR >= 4% to continue. ZeptoLab: CTR > 2.5%, CPI < $0.30 on a basic build (US iOS). [verified] gameanalytics.com early-testing-strategies page (vendor blog).
- Supersonic's version: 6 days, $300, CPI bands above; learning phase takes 3-4 days [verified].
- Cost to us: creatives are agent-made (about $0 cash). Media is the cost: $200-300 per concept [verified, as quoted]; a CAS.AI article says publishers do it in 2-4 days for $300-500 [claimed]; a tier-1 Android marketability test at $600-1,200 [claimed, gamegrowthadvisor]. Espresso reported Facebook Android CPI averaging $2.5 for projects entering its platform in 2021 [claimed], so the $0.25 CPI bar is hard for an unknown title.
- Fit to our caps [assumed]: a $75 micro-test (about $15/day x 5 days) reads CTR only (roughly 200-300 clicks at an assumed $10 CPM and 2.5-4% CTR). Use it as a pre-filter, then let Supersonic fund the CPI test.
- Real-world case: Move People (solo dev with a day job) tested on Supersonic's self-serve platform; CPI fell to $0.35-0.40 after a creative change and about $0.30 after build; D1-ish retention 37-40%; reached top 3 iOS/Android. Revenue and deal terms undisclosed [verified, self-reported vendor case study, 2021]. Chain Cube: publisher-rejected prototype, rebuilt by the developer, about $500k in first scaling months, 20M+ downloads [verified, article 2021-07-26].

## 3. UA-loop model (mobile)
Inputs: tier-3 Android CPI claimed ranges India $0.08-0.30, LATAM $0.15-0.60, SEA $0.20-0.60; US Android $1.50-3.50; iOS about 3-4x Android [claimed, low-confidence, gamegrowthadvisor]. Global gaming CPI $0.56, up 30% YoY (Adjust via vendor blogs) [claimed]. Liftoff: casual D30 ROAS 47% iOS / 15% Android; hyper-casual D7 ROAS about 7.6-7.8% [verified/claimed via liftoff.ai/?p=35616 which cites Segwise]; "ad-driven games need returns within about 30 days" [verified as quoted]. Tier-3 is used for tests because installs are cheap and gameplay/retention can be read, but eCPM is 3-10x lower than tier 1 [claimed], so tier-3 ROAS understates tier-1 payback per install and cannot be extrapolated. Apple Search Ads, Unity Ads and TikTok per-country costs: could not verify (no usable sources).

Loop table (all inputs [assumed] except where noted; ARPDAU = impressions/DAU x eCPM/1000; LTV30 = ARPDAU x cumulative active days in 30 days):
| | Worst | Base | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPI (tier-3 Android) | $0.30 | $0.12 | $0.08 |
| D1 / D7 retention | 25% / 3% | 33% / 6% | 40% / 10% |
| Active days in 30 d | 1.8 | 2.4 | 3.2 |
| Ad impressions per DAU | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| Blended eCPM (tier-3) | $2 | $3 | $4 |
| ARPDAU | $0.012 | $0.024 | $0.040 |
| LTV30 | $0.02 | $0.058 | $0.128 |
| D30 ROAS | 7% | 48% | 160% |
| Verdict | stop at $75 | do not scale; use for data and to pitch | scale: each $1 returns $1.60 in 30 d |
Reading: only the best case clears ROAS 100% by D30, in line with Liftoff's benchmark that average casual cohorts do not [verified]. Therefore the plan treats own-funded UA as a measurement tool, and uses a publisher's UA balance (and its better monetization/mediation) for scale. Best-case compounding [assumed]: $300 test -> $480 at D30 -> reinvest; after 3 cycles about $1,200 of media, i.e. a 90-day horizon, only if CPI holds at scale (it normally rises; Chain Cube's CPI rose to $3-5 US as it scaled [verified]).

Steam wishlist arithmetic [assumed from claimed inputs]: cost per wishlist $1.00-2.50 (one solo case: $4,365 spend, about 4,000 wishlists, $1.10 average, Reddit-led [verified, wnhub 2024]); day-1 wishlist-to-sale median about 12% [claimed]; so about $9 of ad spend per first-day sale. A $5-10 game nets less than that after the platform cut, so Steam wishlist ads are not a self-funding loop for a cheap game. 68-88% of Next Fest wishlists come from people who never played the demo [claimed]; median wishlists during a fest about 800-900, 322 for games under 1,000 pre-fest wishlists [claimed, Zukowski survey]. Next Fest Feb 22-Mar 1 2027, registration closes Jan 10 2027; June 14-21 2027 [claimed, steampageanalyzer; confirm on Steamworks]. Each game gets one Next Fest ever. Keymailer pricing: two reviews disagree ($24.99-299/mo plus campaigns from $249 vs free-$99/mo and $999 bundles); buying moved to Partnier; Woovit pricing could not verify [claimed].

Hyper-casual prototype spec: one-mechanic, 30-60 s loop, rewarded-ad hook, first 10 levels, tutorial-free, D1 target 35-40% and 13% D7 (Clap Clap bar [verified as quoted]) but expect less. Web spec: loads under 3 s, portal-agnostic wrapper (Playgama Bridge), mute/pause hooks, no external links.

## 5. Pitch kit (one folder per game, agent-generated)
1. One-line hook + genre comp ("like X, but Y") and why the mechanic is original.
2. 30-45 s gameplay video, plus 4-8 ad-style cuts (9:16 and 16:9).
3. Playable link (web) or APK; build size; engine.
4. Data table: our CTR, tier-3 CPI, D1/D7, session length, plays by portal (from layers 1-5).
5. Roadmap: 10 levels now, 50 in 6 weeks, monetization plan (rewarded first, no forced interstitial spam: Chain Cube lesson [verified]).
6. Ask: "fund a CPI test"; deal sought: non-exclusive, term-limited, IP retained, UA recoup capped, kill clause. Disclose AI tooling honestly (Poki requires showing process [verified]).
7. Owner bio and one-line studio description. Owner sends emails and forms (owner-only action); outreach is permitted by the owner.

## 6. 90-day plan (Jan 1 - Mar 31 2027)
Runway Oct-Dec: build 3 web prototypes and 6 ad-style creative sets; build pitch kit template; read CrazyGames Terms and Poki guides; set up a contact list (Supersonic, Kwalee, Playgama, Armor Games, 10 license sites).
- Weeks 1-2: ship prototype 1 to CrazyGames Basic Launch and itch; submit all 3 concepts to Supersonic (video form) and Kwalee Hitseeker; owner sends 10 license pitches. Milestone: 1 portal live, 3 publisher submissions.
- Weeks 3-6: portal metrics (CrazyGames Basic -> Full; stage-gate thresholds 200 players, 25% D1, 4 min); Poki fit test on best build; micro fake-ad test on 2 concepts (<= $75 total). Milestone: first dollar of ad share or license offer; at least one concept with CTR >= 2.5-4%.
- Weeks 7-10: act on publisher replies (accept a funded test; negotiate non-exclusive, IP retained); if none, $100-200 own tier-3 test with owner approval. Milestone: one funded CPI reading or one license sale.
- Weeks 11-13: kill or double down: reinvest first revenue in the next prototype plus creatives; decide Steam only if a clear fit; red-team review before any gate promotion. Milestone: cash >= start, one pitch with real data.

## 7. Reinvestment loop (tycoon loop) [all assumed]
Revenue sources feed one pool: portal ad share + license fees + any publisher royalty. Rule: 50% to next prototype/creative tests, 30% to micro-UA, 20% to reserve until Gate 4. Break-even for the studio: 1 license sale ($300-1,000) covers 4-12 micro tests.
| Scenario (90 days) | Assumptions | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Worst | No publisher reply; portals reject or give 500 plays; no license sale | $0-50 revenue; spend <= $75; learn which concepts fail |
| Base | 1-2 portal games reach Full Launch at about 20-50k plays/mo each (EUR 1.20/1,000 plays, an old [claimed] datapoint); 1 license sale $300-600; 1 publisher test run | $100-700 cash; one concept with real CPI data |
| Best | Supersonic or similar funds a test; CPI <= $0.30 and D1 >= 35%; signed non-exclusive deal; publisher funds UA | Publisher-funded scale; our cash not at risk; royalty income begins |

## 8. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Publisher terms unpublished; possible exclusivity, long recoup | Ask for non-exclusive, capped recoup, IP retained, 90-day kill clause; get terms in writing; never sign without owner review |
| AI-built games may be judged unoriginal (Poki, CrazyGames reject clones) | Distinct theme and art direction, disclose tooling, human playtest before submission |
| Own-UA cannot beat ROAS 100% (Liftoff average) | Use UA only as measurement; scale with publisher money |
| Tier-3 data does not transfer to tier 1 | Treat as retention/gameplay signal only; require tier-1 retest by publisher |
| Platform fees and accounts (Play 12-tester rule, Steam 30-day wait) | Start web-first; Steam/Play only after web gate |
| Scam or fee-charging "publishers" and key-sellers | Never pay a publisher; only buy media directly; verify buyer identity before sharing source |

## Verdict: SUPPORTS (as a distribution route), weakens any own-funded UA plan
The best leverage for $500 and no audience is borrowed: free portals, publisher-funded CPI tests ($300 at Supersonic), license fees. Evidence is thin on terms and acceptance rates and I found no AI-specific publisher rule.

## What would change my mind
- Supersonic/Kwalee/Homa terms showing recoupment or exclusivity that makes the deal worse than self-publishing.
- A rejection pattern for AI-built games, or portal acceptance data showing under 5%.
- A verified license price list showing sub-$100 per game.
- A verified Voodoo or Homa current page stating they no longer accept outside prototypes.
