## 3. Failure modes and verification
- Faked or short-lived revenue: screenshots are easy to fake; traffic spikes (direct/social) easier to fake than organic [claimed, investors.club]. Flippa buyer-loss story: a $1,300 purchase listed at $350/mo (price ~4x monthly profit was the tell) and a ~$10k purchase whose traffic fell after an app-trend ended; two later purchases paid back in about 10 months each [verified as a seller-written Flippa blog post, so n=4, survivorship-biased].
- Search dependence: broker guide says Dec 2025 core update hit affiliate sites hard (71% reported drops) and AI Overviews cut CTR ~half on informational queries [claimed, vendor/broker sources; not checked against Google/Seer primary data].
- Platform dependence: listing 3 cannot transfer ad accounts or gateways; Etsy/Amazon account transfer is a ToS issue [claimed].
- Unmaintained: listing 1 has had no new content for a year.
- Checks to demand (norms, [claimed]): Stripe/PayPal/Shopify read-only access or live screen-share; Google Analytics and Search Console access; bank deposits matching payouts; domain registrar/hosting transfer via Escrow.com or FlippaPay; do not pay outside escrow.
- Hard data on success/failure rates of small purchases: **could not find**. No survey, no sell-through for sub-$10k, only anecdotes. Stated as a gap.

## 4. Workload (hours/week after purchase)
All figures are seller-written listing claims [claimed] and often assume staff leaving at sale: dropship stores 1-3 h/wk (organic, listing) to 15-20 h/wk (high-revenue, ads) ; content site 2-3 h/wk with VAs; small SaaS 1 h/wk to ~5 h/month. AI leverage [assumed]: high for content refresh, listing copy, support replies, code fixes for micro-SaaS; low for paid-ads management, supplier issues, and anything needing a payment-processor or AdSense account in the owner's name.

## 5. Financing and structure
Flippa guidance: earn-outs about 70% upfront; seller financing 60-80% down [claimed, Flippa blog]. Seller notes are usually interest-free per a broker [claimed]. ## VERDICT: **Weakens** (buying as the *first* move at $500; unclear at $5k+)
- At $500 (about $250 deployable after the reserve) there is nothing credible to buy; the sub-$2k tier is auction lottery tickets with hidden reserves and template-bait listings.
- At $5-10k, there are listings with seller-reported, platform-"verified" profit at 0.6-1.4x annual profit. That is 2-8x cheaper than sold-deal benchmarks, which signals adverse selection. Plausible but unproven, and it needs 100%+ of the owner's current capital in one non-diversified bet.
- Buying does beat building on one dimension: it skips the no-audience problem if the asset has organic demand. It loses on downside, since a bad purchase burns the cash while a bad build burns hours.
- Top 3 types if pursued: (A) micro-SaaS/tool with Stripe-verified MRR, $2-10k; (B) store with Shopify/WooCommerce data access and an owned email list, $5-10k; (C) aged content site where the buyer gets Search Console + AdSense history, only below ~$3k because of traffic risk.
- This path needs owner-only actions (accounts, bids, seller contact) and, by the charter, an approved gate; it does not fit the G2 $25 cap, so it needs an explicit exception.

## What would change my mind
- Evidence that a majority of $2-10k Data-Verified listings close near ask and pay back within ~12 months (from watchlist or any buyer cohort data).
- A seller who gives read-only processor access, 12 months of deposits matching bank records, and a transferable ad/affiliate account for a $2-5k asset.
- Evidence that build-from-scratch tests (other briefs) show no paid demand within 30 days.
