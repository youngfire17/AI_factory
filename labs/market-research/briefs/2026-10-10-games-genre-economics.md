# Games: genre economics map (2026-10-10)

Analyst: research-analyst. Retrieval date for all links: 2026-10-10.

Tags:
- [verified] = I read the page myself on that date. Tracker estimates are labelled "tracker est." because the tracker itself models them.

## 0. Headline findings and gaps

- **Steam is top-heavy.**
  - Of ~12k releases in H1 2026, median gross was $196 and 67% earned under $1,000; 145 titles (1.2%) passed $1M. [claimed: WN Hub summary of a Steam revenue report; paywalled past the intro]
  - A second report puts the 2026 median at $1,728 as of Sep 1. The two medians differ (different method and cutoff). [claimed: WN Hub]
  - Lowest medians are racing, puzzle, arcade and platformer, at about $1,000 or less. Highest share of games reaching $100k: sandbox 27.3%, FPS 21.6%, RPG 19.4%. [claimed: We Love It report via WN Hub]
  - About 20.3k Steam releases in 2025. [verified: howtomarketagame.com, VGInsights count; other trackers show 20.0k to 20.6k]
- **Steam Page Analyzer rule of thumb.** 1,000 reviews is roughly $150k gross, and Boxleiter estimates can be off by 30-50%. [verified: steampageanalyzer.com niche page and blog; tracker est.]
- **Mobile money is big but in puzzle, strategy and RPG, and it is bought with UA spend.**
  - H1 2026 IAP: strategy $9.3bn, puzzle $8.1bn (up ~20% y/y), RPG $6.1bn, casino $5bn. [claimed: PocketGamer.biz citing Sensor Tower]
  - Puzzle was 33% of genre ad spend. [claimed: same source]
  - Puzzle was 53% of mobile game ad revenue in Jan-May 2026; top ad earners were Block Blast ($127M), Vita Mahjong ($90M) and Candy Crush ($81M). [claimed: GameDevReports on Sensor Tower]
  - Ad-supported games were 84.1% of downloads. [claimed: same]
  - The top 1,000 games' complement earns 29% of ad revenue but 9% of IAP. [claimed: same]
  - Hybrid-casual is the growth segment; hyper-casual economics are weak. [claimed: PocketGamer.biz]
- **Not found.**
  - GDC 2026 State of the Industry has no genre-profitability data. It reports 52% of respondents think generative AI harms the industry, and 36% use it. [claimed: press release coverage]
  - I found no 2026 GameDiscoverCo, Deconstructor of Fun or Zukowski piece naming "underserved genres". GameDiscoverCo data is largely paywalled.
  - I found no Next Fest genre data (Oct 2026 Next Fest runs Oct 19-26). [claimed]
  - I found no verified revenue for small-team Roblox tycoon or simulator games.
- **Closest "underserved" signals** (indirect only):
  - "Cozy" as a capsule keyword among games with >$100k lifetime revenue rose from 0.4% (2022) to 3.1% (2025). This shows growth, not an assessment of saturation. [verified: Outlook Respawn article on GameDiscoverCo data, 2026-02-16]
  - GameDiscoverCo (older, undated) found low PC-gamer hype for "easy-to-make" genres (2D platformer, top-down and twin-stick shooter). [claimed: search snippet]
  - Alinea calls sandbox the best saturation-to-profit balance for 2026. [claimed: mirror site]

## 2. Platform notes

- **Roblox.** Roblox reports $1.7B in DevEx payouts for the 12 months to Jun 30, 2026. The top 10 averaged $65.7M, the top 100 $10.8M, the top 1,000 $1.4M, and the median creator ~$1,500. [claimed: news coverage of Roblox's developer-conference figures]. DevEx rates are from third-party blogs [claimed]. Discovery for a no-audience new game is not verified. I did not find verified revenue for small tycoon or simulator teams.
- **Steam.** Valve takes 30% on the first $10M. Refunds run ~10-15%, and the effective take is ~40-45% of US list price. [claimed: steampageanalyzer blog, estimates]
- **Web portals.** Poki's own 50/50 share is indicative and the real terms are in the agreement [verified: Poki docs]. Third-party earnings ranges ($200-2,000/mo "middle") come from one vendor blog (Cinevva), which sells publishing services [claimed, low confidence].

Reasoning for the key scores:
- **Idle (3 money):** one dev-reported hit with $4,800 in 5 days, which is small but is evidence of strangers paying. The 3 for discoverability reflects one case where Steam's own F2P lists did the work. It is not a repeatable signal.
- **Deckbuilder (1 saturation):** tag releases have grown about 13x since 2020 and are still up 93% this year. The cohort that reaches 1,000 reviews is concentrated at $10+ prices, which implies a content bar we may not meet.
- **Puzzle (2 money):** Steam medians are low and the mobile pool is incumbent-dominated. It scores 5 on buildability and testability because rules and solvers are exactly what AI does well.
- **Survivors-like (2 art, 1 saturation):** the hit examples are from 2021-2022 and the trend has already cooled [claimed].
- **Mobile (g):** discoverability is 1 because the money follows UA spend, which we cannot afford.

## 4. Top 5 genre + platform combinations for a no-audience AI studio

1. **Idle / incremental on Steam F2P (cosmetic DLC) plus a free browser build.**
   - Cheapest test: ship a free browser build to itch.io (owner publishes) and measure the G3 metrics: 200 players, 25% D1 return, 4-minute median session, any tip or purchase signal.
   - Gate on the evidence: a Steam F2P test needs Steam's app fee (I could not verify it today, so [assumed] ~$100). That may exceed the $75 G3 cap and would need owner approval.
2. **Idle / roguelite tower defense, web first.**
   - Cheapest test: a 10-minute-loop web prototype on itch.io. Compare D1 return and wave-milestone reach against a plain idle control build.
3. **Daily puzzle or word game on the open web (own domain or itch.io).**
   - Cheapest test: a deterministic daily-seed puzzle with a shareable result string. Measure return visits at day 2 and day 7.
   - Treat portals (CrazyGames, Poki) as a later submission. Poki is closed beta and CrazyGames terms could not be verified.
4. **Small-scope roguelike deckbuilder or poker hybrid, itch.io demo first, Steam only if the demo retains.**
   - Cheapest test: a 20-minute demo with a single strong hook. Only proceed if the demo clears the retention thresholds, since this is the most crowded option in the top 5.
5. **Survivors-like or roguelite arcade on itch.io / web, as a retention test.**
   - Cheapest test: a 15-minute-run build. Do not go to Steam unless D1 return is at least 25% and the hook is distinct. Treat as lowest-priority of the five, given the crowding and the art load.

Roblox is not in the top 5. The hit-and-median data is top-heavy, there is no verified small-team evidence, and discovery needs an audience we do not have.

## 5. What would change my mind

- A verified (primary or tracker) median for the survivors-like or idle tag showing a healthy share above $10k.
- Direct evidence that portal approval (Poki, CrazyGames) is open to AI-built games and what it pays.
- Verified revenue for small-team tower defense, cozy, or tycoon games (none found).
- Evidence that Steam's AI-disclosure policy or portal curation penalizes AI-built titles (I did not check this; it matters for all combos).
- A repeatable second case of a dev-built idle game earning in its first weeks without marketing, to replace the single anecdote.

## 6. Verdict and next cheapest test

- **Verdict:** unclear for "commercially successful games with $500". The evidence supports small cheap tests in idle, daily puzzle and TD. It does not support any genre as proven for a no-audience studio. Steam medians are low across the board.
- **Next cheapest test:** one browser build (idle), with a deterministic sim bot as the acceptance test, put in front of ~200 strangers via itch.io. Cost $0.
- **Needed from the owner:** an itch.io account and publishing, which are owner-only actions. I have not contacted anyone or created anything.
